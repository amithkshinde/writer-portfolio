What is Representation of People Act, 1951? How was the disqualification of the sitting member decided? Let us find out.

Before diving into the ROP Act, 1951, We must observe what the Constitution of India states under the Article 102(1)(e).

“(1) A person shall be disqualified for being chosen as, and for being, a member of either House of Parliament”
(e) “if he is so disqualified by or under any law made by Parliament Explanation For the purposes of this clause a person shall not be deemed to hold an office of profit under the Government of India or the Government of any State by reason only that he is a Minister either for the Union or for such State”

The Representation of People Act, 1951 is an act to provide for the conduct of elections of the Houses of Parliament and to the House/Houses of the Legislature of each state. This includes all the qualifications and disqualifications involved in the electoral process, post-election and any dispute arising to the conduct of both election and candidate. This act was tabled by the then Law Minister of the country, Dr. Bheemrao Ambedkar with a special provision under Article 327 of the Indian Constitution before 1951. The Section 1 to Section 5 of this Act mainly talks about the conduct of elections for the parliament, state legislatures and on the whole electoral process including the registration of the political parties. Section 6 and Section 7 takes us through the disputes during the electoral process and with the Corrupt Practices and Electoral Offences. Let us walk through our focal point today, the Section 8 of the Representation of People’s Act, 1951 which is reserved for the disqualifications of the elected members on conviction of certain offences. Let us further drill down to the sub-section (3) of the Act, which states,

“(3) A person convicted of any offence and sentenced to imprisonment for not less than two years other than any offence referred to in sub-section (1) or sub-section (2)] shall be disqualified from the date of such conviction and shall continue to be disqualified for a further period of six years since his release.]”

So based on the observations it is evident from section8(3) of the act that any member found guilty will face minimum of 8 years of suspension from contesting any active elections. Yes, you read it right, Rahul Gandhi will not be able to contest any elections till 2031 if proven guilty in the top courts.

The Lily Thomas Verdict

If you closely observe the Representation of People Act, 1951 the sub-section 4 gives a buffer time of three months to the convicted member for his repeal in the higher court or the application of revision with respect to the conviction or the sentence disposed from the court. The buffer time of three months implies the convicted can use all his power to make sure the decision reverts either by his/her appeal further in the top courts, or pleading for the sentence to be reduced less than 2 years, which eventually reprimands his/her disqualification of MP. Lily Thomas, Kerala based advocate had petitioned against the amendment of the section 8(4) late in 1952. But then it was not given any scope by the top court. Lily Thomas aged 77 along with Adv. Satya Narian Shukla again brought in a petition in the apex court to repeal the act in 2005. After 8 years of Arguments, On 10th July 2013 the divisional bench of Justice AK Patnaik and SJ Mukhopadhaya of the Supreme court gave a historical mandate naming the section 8(4) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951 as Unconstitutional. The bench stated quote “Parliament had no power to enact sub-section (4) of Section 8 of the Act and accordingly sub-section (4) of Section 8 of the Act is ultra vires the Constitution.” quote ends. The above judgement from bench meant that the disqualification of the member will be applicable from the day he was pronounced as convicted from any court under the jurisdictions of the constitution. This is the judgement which made a severe impact on the Indian Electoral Politics, thanks to Lily Thomas and Adv. SN Shukla.

The Ordinance of 2013

The then UPA government was quick to act on this landmark judgement from the supreme court. Kapil Sibal, law minister in the Manmohan Singh government introduced “The Representation of People (Second Amendment and Validation) Bill, 2013 on 30th of August 2013. The Bill went to amend the rules under the section8(4) of the act not to disqualify the member immediately after the date of sentence by the court. The Indian government filed a review petition in the top court to overturn the judgement. The Supreme Court’s stand on the case was completely made clear when the review petition of the government was ruthlessly quashed within no time. The RJD which was the ally of the UPA in the government had pressurized the Manmohan Singh government to bring in the bill as soon as possible, as the Fodder Scam Verdict was around the corner in the top court. The prime accused in the fodder scam was RJD supremo Lalu Prasad Yadav. The arguments were completed and only the judgement was pending. Whole country knew that Lalu would be convicted under the law, but were only waiting to see how years of imprisonment will be given for the former railway minister. Now we can connect the dots as to why the RJD wanted the government to act immediately. Apparently, the UPA government did not waste any time to bring the bill into effect as an ordinance on 24th September 2013. This ordinance protected the convicted from suffering any kind of disqualification for the first three months after the verdict was given. The Congress prince Rahul Gandhi took the stage on Gandhi Jayanthi to showcase his personal strength and announce himself on the global stage for the upcoming elections of 2014. He had called a press meet on the same day and tore the copy of the ordinance and condemned his own government at the highest possible level. They claimed that the ordinance was non-sense and did harm to the Indian Democracy. This ensured the outrage of all the UPA allies, forget about the allies, even the Congress top brass was sent to trance following RaGa’s move.
