After retaining the Border-Gavaskar Trophy at home, India were hopeful of defeating the Aussies in the 50-over format. But the visitors looked to be the better side for the most part of the ODI series, as they thrashed the hosts by a 2-1 margin. This loss meant that India had lost an ODI series at home for the first time since March 2019 before the World Cup. Even though this can be brushed under the carpet as a one-off event, there is more to it than we think. Here are some of the key takeaways from the series:

1. King Kohli is back

This is one of the few positive things that the Indian team will feel good about, leading to the 2023 World Cup. Virat Kohli is tailor made for this format but his declining numbers were a cause of concern, as he averaged 33.52 in 25 ODIs between his 43rd and 44th ODI century (From 15th of August 2019 to 9th of December 2022). Even though there were impactful innings from his willow (10 fifties), a string of low scores and uncharacteristic dismissals did not help his cause. The turnaround was inevitable for the run machine, as he brought up centuries against Bangladesh and Sri Lanka and was looking at his fluent best. Even that knock at Chennai against Australia on a slow turner, spoke volumes about his ability to soak in the pressure. We have already seen glimpses of the Kohli of the yesteryears, who is known to rotate strike and go ballistic in the backend. The only question is, if our best batsman can turn up on the biggest stage in October.

2. The batting order conundrum

The World Cup is less than six months away but the Indian team management has not been able to figure out the best eleven possible. Even though the injuries of Rishabh Pant, Jasprit Bumrah and Shreyas Iyer came in as a surprise, the Rohit-Dravid era has experimented quite a lot in the past few years, but have failed to zero in on the players. The prospect of opening with Gill and Rohit sounds exciting, but the former player needs to make technical adjustments against lateral movement while the skipper needs to convert his explosive starts to daddy hundreds.

Coming to the middle order, it is 2019 all over again as we do not have a fixed candidate for Number 4. Since the 2019 World Cup, India has tried 8 different players at No.4 with Iyer and Pant topping the list, having played 22 and 11 innings respectively. The next player, KL Rahul has played just 3 innings at No.4 while Ishan Kishan, Suryakumar Yadav and Manish Pandey have not justified their spot yet. The absence of Pant also means that our batting order does not have a left hander to counter the bowling attack. One of the options to get out of this mess, is to play someone like Sundar at 4 so that the likes of KL, Hardik and Jadeja can capitalize in the last 15 overs. Even though this is highly unlikely, given that the team has put their weight behind Suryakumar Yadav, a left hander at four can make wonders.

3. India’s woes against lateral movement

The concerns of Indian top order collapsing against seam movement is pronounced than ever, as they failed miserably against Starc and co in the entire ODI series. Even though there is enough data to suggest that it is due to the new batch of Kookaburra balls that is offering extra movement off the air and through the pitch, the Indian openers have looked all at sea when the ball jags in or goes away. In order to minimize the risk and maximize the reward, the likes of Rohit Sharma and Gill should cash in on the looseners while not committing to the drive/cut on a fuller length delivery.

4. Australia are the firm favorites

The Australians have once again proven why they are one of the most competitive sides going around, with an emphatic victory over India in the LOIs. The magnitude of the victory should not be underplayed, as they were without the services of Pat Cummins, Josh Hazlewood and Jhye Richardson. Here the captaincy of Steven Smith should be appreciated, as he promoted Mitch Marsh up the order and the returns were there for everyone to see. He scored at a jaw-dropping strike rate of 131.08 whilst also averaging 97 in three games. On the other hand, David Warner could be dropped to Number 4, to tackle spinners in the middle overs, with muscle power of Maxwell, Stoinis and Carey to follow. Looking at their spin attack, the duo of Zampa and Agar is looking like a deadly combination because of their control and accuracy, which we witnessed in Chennai. The spinners gave away 86 runs in 20 overs and scalped 6 wickets in the process. Coming to the pace battery, Mitchell Starc is a dangerous customer in ICC tournaments and he has already shown us glimpses of what he can do, when the ball starts moving. Overall, the Australians are looking like a formidable opposition heading into the World Cup.
