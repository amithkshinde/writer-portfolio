Resuming from where we left off, we will be covering two major districts of North Karnataka, one from the Kittur Karnataka region and one more from the Kalyana Karnataka region, Bidar and Raichur. The Land of famous Bidri art, house for multiple architectural marvels of the Indo-Saracenic style constructed under the Bahamani Sultanate and Adil Shahi rule, Bidar is the topmost district of Karnataka sharing the borders with Maharashtra and Telangana. Raichur district is renowned for owning, one of the few active gold mines in India. The whole of Vijayanagara Kingdom was built around the banks of river Krishna and river Tungabhadra, which is called as the Raichur Doab. The territory of Raichur and Bidar has not only seen historical battles but also some mouthwatering political battles post 1947. Mostly known for the battles between individual families, the districts have 13 Vidhana Sabha constituencies in total under their name. The perfect example of triangular fight can be seen in these districts. The Janata Dal which is known as the 3 and half zilla party in the Karnataka politics has made a commendable impact on the voters of this region to gain their confidence. Out of the 13 constituencies the grand old party has 7 seats, whereas the BJP and JDS have 3 representatives each. The trends on the upcoming elections have been discussed below.

Raichur

The heat wave production isn’t only restricted to Shakti Nagar, with elections around the corner it has reached every street and colony of Raichur. The 7 constituencies of the district are divided among the BJP, Congress and JDS with two, three and two seats respectively. The Raichur district on the whole, contains multiple establishments including paddy production, gold mines, thermal power plants and many more.The Loksabha seat of Raichur is a reserved constituency and has the majority of the population belonging to the Naik community, which houses 4 ST reserved Vidhana Sabha constituencies. The reserved constituency of Raichur Rural and Lingasugur tends to vote in the same way and has always favored the Congress Party. But with the JDS stalwart Manappa Vajjal joining the BJP, the Lingasugur constituency is all set to select a BJP candidate after a decade. Basangouda Daddal of the congress has placed himself comfortably against BJP’s Tipparaju Hawaldar. The JDS stronghold constituencies Manvi and Sindhanur will go for polls with same set of candidates when compared to the previous elections. Current MLA Raja Venkatappa Nayaka in Manvi of the JDS has made his field clear in the constituency to hit a winning goal again in 2023 elections. Runner-up of the 2018 elections Hampanagouda Badarli of Congress party from the Sindhanur constituency might have the edge over the current MLA Venkatarao Nadagouda in the upcoming polls. Several corruption allegations against the JDS MLA have led to a shrink in his popularity compared to the previous elections. In both the constituencies BJP doesn’t have its presence in the mainstream. Maski which is a ST reserved constituency went for the polls twice in last 5 years. Pratapgouda Patil, winner of the 2018 polls rebelled against Congress and joined BJP. His opponent Basangowda Turvihal who had contested from the BJP swapped the parties in 2021. Turvihal won the by-polls and was successful in defeating Pratapgouda Patil, who has the backing of major Lingayat community in the constituency. In 2023, both Patil and Turvihal will go head-to-head and the wave seem to be towards the Saffron party.

Bidar

The Khandres, the Nagamarapallis, the Patils, the Beldaale’s, the Khuba’s you name it, the whole politics of Bidar district has been vested under few families. The industries, schools, colleges, and each of the state functioning entities will have either of the above-mentioned name before them. The population is mainly based on agriculture and shares its border prominently with Maharashtra and the influence is there to be seen. Bidar has 6 constituencies and the Congress is well established in the region with 4 out of 6 seats in the district. Basavakalyana taluk is all set to witness a fight between BJP’s Sharanu Salagar and JDS candidate Mallikarjun Khuba. Basavakalyana went for the polls twice, due to the demise of their MLA B Narayan Rao of the Congress. Runner-up BJP candidate in the 2018 elections Mallikarjun Khuba was not given the ticket in the by-polls of 2021. BJP placed its bet on the winning knight Sharanu Salagar. Mallikarjun Khuba contested as Independent, but failed to make any impact in the by-polls. Now, Mallikarjun Khuba is back to JDS, from where he had won the seat of Basavakalyana in 2013. Sharanu Salagar is well equipped and will offer a best fight against the JDS candidate. JDS will be expecting the emotional wave of two consecutive loss to work for Mallikarjun Khuba. Congress might field the runner-up of the last election Mala Narayana Rao. It’ll never be claimed false if call the Humnabad constituency as the Castle of Patils. A member from the Patil family has won 9 out 13 elections held in Humnabad since independence. Carrying the legacy is Rajshekhar Patil, who won the last 3 elections from the constituency. He also held the ministry of Mines, Commerce and Industries in the Congress-JDS coalition government. There seem to no opposition for him in the upcoming polls too. In 2018, he won with a thumping margin of 30,000+ votes. BJP and JDS are still in search of an able candidate against Rajshekhar Patil. One more family bastion led seat in the district is Bidar Rural. The Nagamarapallis had established themselves to be the stakeholders of the taluk, but the minority population who has the decisive votes in the constituency has never let the Saffron party to let their foot in since 1999. Gurupadappa Nagamarapalli has won twice here, but never from BJP. He won the seat in 2008 contesting the elections from Congress. In 2009, he defected to BJP and contested from the same seat but was not successful. In 2013, Gurupadappa contested from KJP and secured a comfortable win over Congress candidate Rahim Khan. Current MLA Rahim Khan will see a formidable opposition being formed from Suryakant Nagamarapalli. With bringing the minority votes into count, Rahim Khan might once again win from the taluk. Mostly populated by the Lambani population, Aurad ticks all the points for the BJP to achieve its mission. Prabhu Chawan, currently the minister of Animal Husbandry has made Hindutva as his primary agenda in the elections. Congress here is still confused on its candidate due to the ticket dispute between Bhimrao Shindhe and Arali Kumar.

Constituencies to watch out for

Bhalki

A seat known for the pride of Congress party and to the lineage of Khandres, the constituency of Bhalki is one more product of family politics. Post the formation of the Karnataka state in 1973, except in 1985 every election here in Bhalki are won by the Khandre house. They’ve formed a monopoly over the seat of Bhalki. Starting from Bhimanna Khandre in 1978, Vijaykumar Khandre in 1989 and to the third product Eshwar Khandre in 2008 everyone in the family is elected minimum twice in their political career. Prakash Khandre, tall leader in the Saffron party has been elected twice from the constituency in 1999 and 2004. Later on, suffered consecutive losses against Eshwar Khandre and was away from the competitive politics. DK Sidram, local leader of BJP rose pretty quickly in the party and was given a chance to contest in the 2018 elections. DK Sidram was unable to control the force of Eshwar Khandre. Eshwar Khandre was once the state president of congress, but after DK Shivakumar came into the fray, he was replaced and created a new position of working president. Now in 2023, Prakash Khandre is all set to contest the elections again from BJP. Runner-up in the previous election DK Sidram has also agreed with the party’s top brass and joined hands with Prakash Khandre. Both Sidram and Prakash coming together might give an edge for the BJP, but assessing the stature of Eshwar Khandre, you can never back him out of the game. The prestigious seat of Bhalki can be weighed as the contest between the heavyweights. If BJP be able to make inroads in Bhalki and defeat Eshwar Khandre, it’ll be a landmark victory for the Saffron party.

Bidar South

It was 2013, the stage was set for a triangular fight between the three major parties of the state. But out of nowhere, walks in a candidate named Ashok Kheny to win the seat of Bidar South. He had contested from his own party, Karnataka Makkala Paksha. The JDS was silenced with their key leader Bandeppa Kashempur losing to Ashok Kheny in a multi front battle in the 2013 elections. Bidar South has always been a stronghold of the JDS in north Karnataka. It was once said that the JDS executes its strategies of North Karnataka right from the constituency of Bidar South. They went on prove the same in 2018. Bandeppa Kashempur was re-elected from the constituency. In 2006, when Siddaramaiah left the JDS, he had complained that the Gowda family will never let any Kuruba leader to grow in their party. But Bandeppa Kashempur is an absolute contrast to Sidda’s claim. He was given ministry twice under Kumaraswamy in both 2008 and 2018. Bandeppa Kashempur has been a loyalist to the Gowda family and has done his fair bit in maintaining the face of the party in North Karnataka. He jointly led the Mahadayi protest against the then government with Konareddy in Navalagunda. He launched the “Badavara Bandhu” program for the street vendors under Congress-JDS coalition government in 2019. Now, in the 2023 elections, Bidar South has several ups and downs with Ashok Kheny moving to Congress and several organizational changes in the BJP taluk unit. The Beldaale family has regained its lost charm and is seen actively working in the constituency. Will Kashempur be able retain the seat, or will it be Kheny’s magic again in 2023, we need to wait till the 13th of March.
