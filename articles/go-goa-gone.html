Three days is enough to cover the main parts of Goa if you keep each day to one area. This plan is split that way so you are not driving across the state every day. North Goa is the busy one, the south is quieter, and the old churches sit in the middle.

Day 1: North Goa

Start at Fort Aguada, at the mouth of the Mandovi River. The Portuguese built it in 1612. You can walk the walls and see the Arabian Sea from the top.

Sinquerim Beach is right next to the fort, so go there next. Jet skiing and parasailing are available if you want them.

Then head to Calangute for the street markets and some street food. Finish at Baga Beach in the evening. The beach shacks play live music, and the Calangute to Baga stretch is crowded by then, so leave extra time for the road.

Day 2: South Goa

The south is less crowded than the north. Start at Palolem Beach, which has pale sand and calm water. It is a good place to just sit for a few hours.

In the evening go to Margao, the main town of the south. Walk through the market and eat Goan food at one of the local places.

Day 3: Old Goa and Panaji

Old Goa was the capital of Portuguese Goa, and the churches here are close together, so this day is easy to walk. Start with the Basilica of Bom Jesus. It holds the remains of St. Francis Xavier. The Sé Cathedral is close by, and it is one of the largest churches in Asia.

Then see the Church of St. Francis of Assisi. The Basilica, the Sé Cathedral and this church are all part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing for Old Goa.

The Viceroy's Arch dates to 1597. The tower of St. Augustine is what remains of a church built in the early 1600s, and it is still standing among the ruins.

Old Goa is about 10 km from Panaji, so end the day there. The old Latin quarter, Fontainhas, has painted houses and narrow lanes, and the riverfront is good for an evening walk.
